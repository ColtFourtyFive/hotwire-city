# Research notes (2026-10-26)
Gathered by research agents from web sources; tracker numbers are approximate.

## Market & virality

RESEARCH BRIEF (as of 2026-10-26). Most figures come from third-party trackers such as RoWatcher, GGAID and profitable.app, so treat them as approximate.

1) WHAT WENT VIRAL AND WHY
- Grow a Garden peaked at 22.3M concurrent players (CCU) on 2025-08-23. Credited mechanics: crops keep growing while you are offline, weekly limited items you must log in to claim, weather and time mutations, pets, and weekend "admin abuse" events.
- Steal a Brainrot released 2025-05-16 and peaked at 25.4M CCU in Oct 2025. On 2025-08-23 the two games staged an "Admin War" together, and Roblox hit a platform record of 47.4M CCU that day.
  - Steal a Brainrot's loop: buy units off a central conveyor, or physically carry them out of other players' bases. Units on your pedestals earn cash every second. Your base has a temporary lock or shield, you defend with a bat, and you can rebirth.
  - Its virality came from TikTok and YouTube clips of kids reacting to losing their Brainrots, plus memes, events and crossovers.
  - Main criticisms: pay-to-win (admin powers can be bought), auto-steal scripts, and griefing. An anti-griefing patch landed on 2026-01-31.
  - Trackers estimate revenue at $1.4–4.4M/month now and about $11M/month at its peak.
- 99 Nights in the Forest peaked at 14.2M CCU. Dead Rails took off through Roblox's official social posts, then the algorithm's feedback loop, then big YouTubers. Its formula: a familiar format plus one twist.
- 2026 wave:
  - Grow a Garden 2 launched 2026-06-12 and peaked at 976K. Its headline feature is night-only stealing: you must walk stolen goods home with no teleport, and defenses include cactus, bees and a freeze ray. It fell about 66% from July to August.
  - Steal An Egg launched late July 2026 and peaked between 500K and 1.2M. It uses 7-player servers, egg stealing and a treadmill for speed. Roblox briefly removed it on Aug 24–25 because of a "Reels" feed that rewarded watching. That led to a new Roblox rule restricting reward-driven media feeds for under-16 users.
  - Short, impulse-buy formats dominate the live charts: +1 Speed Keyboard Escape (around 358K), Sell Lemons, Kick a Lucky Block.
  - Murder Mystery 2 hit 1.35M CCU in Aug 2026 on the strength of 12 years of limited items.
  - Pattern: meme games have 8–12 minute sessions and decay fast.
- Roblox's discovery algorithm, since June 2026: "Recommended for You" now judges games on 28-day retention (day 1, days 2–7 and days 8–28), with separate signals for play-through, session quality and spend. Viral spikes without retention no longer get boosted.
- Discovery is also age-gated. A Mild maturity label is needed to reach Roblox Kids (ages 5–8); Moderate reaches only Select (9–15) and standard (16+). A new game starts out shown only to age-checked users 16+, and unlocks the under-16 tiers after 250 unique plays from "highly engaged" players within 60 days.

2) GTA-LIKE AND DRIVING GAMES ON ROBLOX
- None is in the top 20.
- Jailbreak: 8.08B visits and an 87.7% like ratio. It peaked at 173K at its Chapter 4 live event on 2026-06-13 and now sits around 5–11K. Players criticise it as stagnant and grind-heavy (hyperchrome grinding), and the developers are focused on anti-cheat.
- Emergency Hamburg: peaked at 76K on 2026-10-17 and is growing about 40–50% over 30 days. It is a realistic police and roleplay sim.
- Driving Empire: peaked at 90K on 2026-08-15 with licensed brand launches (Porsche, Lamborghini, RAM). It has since fallen 31% over 30 days.
- Car Dealership Tycoon: peaked at 36K in Dec 2024 and now has about 5–9K. It has 400+ cars and a 93% like ratio.
- Wanted: peaked at 21K. Mad City: Chapter 1 and 2 each now have only a few hundred players.
- Direct precedent for our concept: "Steal A Car" by By Car Stealers launched Sept 2026 and peaked at 31K CCU in Oct 2026 with 21.7M visits. It already does car stealing, police escapes, a treadmill for speed and stealing from other players.
- Roughly 3,300 "Steal a ___" clones exist.
- Bloomberg (via The Star, 2026-09-30) reports that GTA-inspired Roblox games (Brookhaven, Jailbreak, Da Hood, Wanted) have more than 100B plays combined. GTA VI is expected on 2026-11-19, and analysts predict a wave of Roblox GTA spin-offs. That is a big search and hype window.
- The gap: crime games are either deep-but-stale cops-vs-robbers (Jailbreak) or realistic roleplay sims with long, text-heavy sessions. No game yet combines:
  - the steal-and-collect economy,
  - offline income,
  - server-wide events and mutations,
  - and genuinely fun driving and chases.
  "Steal A Car" proves demand but plateaued around 31K, likely because it is a reskin.

3) VERDICT ON "GRAND THEFT GARAGE"
- Strong concept, but it must not be a Steal-a-Brainrot reskin. The differentiator should be that every steal becomes a getaway chase you drive yourself.
- It needs four things:
  - a Mild, cartoon-crime presentation (non-lethal, no blood, no realistic guns),
  - anti-frustration rules for theft,
  - systems that hold players for 28 days (collection book, contracts, seasons, garage building),
  - small servers (8–12 players) in a compact city.
- Pitfalls:
  - Take-Two trademark risk in the "Grand Theft" name.
  - Real car brands without a licence.
  - Realistic firearms or gore, which push the game to a Moderate or Restricted label.
  - Drugs, alcohol, or anything copying real-world crimes.
  - Reward-driven video feeds.
  - Paid random items without published odds and PolicyService checks.
  - Fake-urgency selling (Roblox rules ban timers that auto-restart and "LAST CHANCE" copy).
  - Exploits from clients owning vehicle physics.
  - Car duplication when a stolen car moves between two players' saves.

4) MONETIZATION AND ONBOARDING
- Monetize with:
  - permanent gamepasses: 2x cash, VIP, faster stealing, extra garage slots, auto-collect;
  - repeatable products: server-wide luck boosts, skip a lock cooldown, fun effects;
  - an 800-Robux season pass;
  - limited weekly cars;
  - weekend events;
  - friend referral rewards;
  - rewarded video ads once the game averages 100K+ daily users.
  Never sell power that ends other players' fun.
- Onboarding: the player must be driving a stolen car within 20–30 seconds and see income within 60 seconds. Keep it visual and mobile-first, and don't rely on chat (age checks gate it).

### Risks
- Saturation: 'Steal A Car' (Sept 2026, 31K peak) and about 3,300 'Steal a ___' clones already exist. A reskin will be lost among them, and lookalike games may get less discovery. The driving and chase twist has to come through clearly in the thumbnail.
- Viral decay: Steal a Brainrot fell from 25.4M CCU to about 130K, and Grow a Garden 2 fell about 66% in a month. Since June 2026 the algorithm judges 28-day retention, so a spike with no depth loses recommendations.
- Maturity: realistic guns, blood, 'death', drugs, alcohol, or recreating real crimes or police violence pushes the game to Moderate or Restricted (losing Kids ages 5–8, or everyone under 18) or breaks Community Standards.
- Trademark and IP: the 'Grand Theft' title and real car makes or logos could draw Take-Two or automaker complaints. Use original naming and parody brands until there is a licence.
- Theft frustration: griefing and losing cars while offline cause downvotes and churn. Steal a Brainrot needed an anti-griefing patch.
- Exploits: clients owning vehicle physics allows speed and teleport hacks, and auto-steal scripts appear quickly. Server Authority was still in beta as of July 2026.
- Data integrity: moving a car between two players' saves risks duplication or loss unless the transfer is atomic and profiles are session-locked.
- Pay-to-win backlash: selling admin powers or direct PvP advantages draws press criticism and lower ratings.
- Platform headwinds: 2026 age checks reportedly cut daily users and gate chat. New games start out 16+-only until they reach 250 highly engaged plays in 60 days, which slows early growth.
- Policy traps: reward-driven media feeds, paid random items without published odds or PolicyService checks, playable gambling, and fake-urgency timers.
- Performance: AI traffic, many vehicles and chases on low-end mobile can hurt session quality. Use small servers, StreamingEnabled and pooling.
- Data reliability: most CCU and revenue figures come from third-party trackers (RoWatcher, GGAID, profitable.app) and conflict across snapshots. No reliable 2026 data was found for Southwest Florida, Greenville, ER:LC or Blue Lock Rivals.

## Engine & tech

Researched against current Creator Hub docs (most pages updated Oct 2026), DevForum announcements, and the engine itself: the v0.744 API dump and PlayerModule source from MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker, plus the rbx-dom reflection DB that Rojo uses. Sound IDs were checked live against the Creator Store and economy APIs; every ID listed is Roblox or licensed-partner owned and IsPublicDomain=true.

Main conclusions:
(1) For an arcade GTA-style car, use a single-body raycast car driven by the driver's client: four per-wheel VectorForces for suspension, mass-scaled accelerations for drive and brake, and direct writes to sideways and yaw velocity for grip and steering. Add an airborne-only AlignOrientation to keep it upright. Read cross-platform input from VehicleSeat.ThrottleFloat/SteerFloat; the current PlayerModule fills these from Input Action System (IAS) actions (VehicleContext.ThrottleAction/SteerAction), so mobile, gamepad and keyboard all work for free. Avoid wheels built from constraints and avoid legacy BodyMovers.
(2) Traffic should be a server-side data simulation over a road graph: lanes, IDM car-following, about 10 Hz. Clients render pooled, anchored, client-local models from compact buffer snapshots. Stealing a car swaps the proxy for a real physics car at the simulated CFrame and velocity. Police are server-owned physics cars on the same chassis module, steered by pure pursuit plus A* on the road graph.
(3) Current API status:
- IAS (InputContext/InputAction/InputBinding) is shipped and used by the default PlayerModule.
- Legacy chat is gone; use TextChatService.
- Sound still works, but docs now steer new work to AudioPlayer/AudioEmitter/Wire. AudioPlayer.AssetId is deprecated in favour of Asset.
- Highlight cap is now 255.
- Server Authority (AuthorityMode=Server plus BindToSimulation) reached full release in July 2026 but adds heavy constraints.
- Rojo-built places default StreamingEnabled=false and SignalBehavior=Default (=Immediate), so set them explicitly.
(4) Use ProfileStore (MadStudioRoblox) for session-locked player data. ProcessReceipt must be idempotent on PurchaseId. Use OrderedDataStore for all-time boards (tight write budget) and MemoryStore SortedMap for daily/weekly boards. Policy: paid random items need full % odds and PolicyService checks; aim for a Mild maturity label (no blood, KO instead of death).
(5) Usable sound IDs exist: Roblox Resources Car-Engine-Loop 80317022777602, ProSoundEffects sirens, horns, cash and crashes, Roblox UI clicks. The client ships only about 11 built-in rbxasset:// sounds.

### Recommendations
- **CAR ARCHITECTURE: Build a single-body raycast car, not one built from constraints (CylindricalConstraint/SpringConstraint/HingeConstraint motors) and not a classic VehicleSeat hinge car. Model layout: invisible PrimaryPart 'Chassis' (about 6x1.6x12 studs, CanCollide=true, CustomPhysicalProperties density 0.7, friction 0.1, elasticity 0) holding the collision hull. A welded VehicleSeat with MaxSpeed=0, Torque=0, TurnSpeed=0, HeadsUpDisplay=false. All visual meshes welded with Massless=true, CanCollide/CanQuery/CanTouch=false. Wheels are visual only, placed by Weld.C0 from the ray hit distance and spun by speed/radius. Add 4 Attachments at the wheel mounts (about ±2.6, -0.2, ±3.8), each with a VectorForce (RelativeTo=World, ApplyAtCenterOfMass=false). Add one center VectorForce 'Drive' (ApplyAtCenterOfMass=true, RelativeTo=World) and one AlignOrientation 'Upright'. Set Model.ModelStreamingMode=Atomic.**
  - Why: Constraint wheels jitter under replication, explode on hard impacts, and are FPS- and ownership-sensitive. Hinge setups are 'very wobbly' per DevForum, and VehicleSeat only drives hinges that are directly attached. One assembly with forces is cheap to replicate (one root CFrame), stable, and fully tunable for an arcade feel. Legacy BodyMovers are tagged Deprecated in the v744 API dump. — https://devforum.roblox.com/t/in-depth-scripted-car-physics/3915628 ; https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/physics/mover-constraints ; https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/reference/engine/classes/VehicleSeat ; Client-Tracker Full-API-Dump v0.744
- **SUSPENSION MATH (all accelerations multiplied by M = Chassis.AssemblyMass, so tuning does not depend on mass; g = workspace.Gravity = 196.2). Per wheel: L = rest + radius (rest 1.6, radius 1.2, so L = 2.8). hit = workspace:Raycast(att.WorldPosition, -up*L, params), where params excludes car and characters via CollisionGroup or FilterDescendantsInstances. comp = L - hit.Distance. vComp = -Chassis:GetVelocityAtPosition(att.WorldPosition):Dot(up). a = clamp(k*comp + c*vComp, 0, 4*g). Force_i = hit.Normal * M * a, or up*M*a. Pick k for about 45% static sag: k = g/(4*0.45*rest) ≈ 68 per wheel, giving body ω = sqrt(4k) ≈ 16.5 rad/s (≈2.6 Hz). Damping c = ζ*ω/2 with ζ 0.4–0.6, so c ≈ 3.5–5 per wheel. No hit means force 0 and the wheel counts as airborne. Lower the center of mass (thin chassis or a low ballast part) and add downforce while grounded: aDown = 0.25*g + 0.002*vF².**
  - Why: These are concrete, mass-independent numbers with known stability margins. The explicit spring is stable while dt*ω < ~1, i.e. down to ≈16 FPS; damping is stable while 4*c*dt < 2. That matters because DevForum raycast cars commonly go bouncy or fling below 30 FPS when tuned with unit-mass constants such as 2500/250. — https://devforum.roblox.com/t/in-depth-scripted-car-physics/3915628 ; https://devforum.roblox.com/t/solvedproblem-with-raycasting-car-in-low-fps/3042743 ; https://devforum.roblox.com/t/raycast-suspension-flings/1691989
- **DRIVE / BRAKE / GRIP / STEER / DRIFT. Apply only when 2 or more wheels are grounded, scaled by the grounded fraction. fwd and right come from Chassis.CFrame projected on the ground plane; v = AssemblyLinearVelocity; vF = v·fwd; vR = v·right.
- Throttle > 0: aDrive = thr * 45 * (1 - (vF/Vmax)²), with Vmax ≈ 130 studs/s (≈36 m/s) and nitro to 170.
- Throttle < 0 while vF > 2: brake at 90 studs/s².
- Otherwise reverse at 25 studs/s², capped at 45 studs/s.
- Coasting: rolling resistance of 6 studs/s², plus air drag of -0.0005*vF*|vF|.
- Grip (arcade): aLat = clamp(-vR*gripRate, ±1.2*g) with gripRate 10/s. Handbrake drops the rear to gripRate 1.5 and lerps back over about 0.4 s, which gives drifting. Award drift points from |vR| and time.
- Set Drive.Force = M*(fwd*(aDrive+aDrag) + right*aLat - up*aDown).
- Steering: write the yaw rate directly on the owning client. targetYaw = -steer * 2.4 * clamp(|vF|/15,0,1) * (1 - 0.45*clamp(|vF|/Vmax,0,1)) * sign(vF). Then yawNow = av:Dot(up) and Chassis.AssemblyAngularVelocity = av + up*((targetYaw - yawNow)*min(1, 8*dt)). SteerFloat +1 means right, and positive Y rotation turns left, hence the minus sign.**

The quadratic falloff gives a soft top speed instead of a hard clamp. — Design synthesis; force-application patterns from https://devforum.roblox.com/t/raycast-vehicle-movement/3640420 and https://devforum.roblox.com/t/in-depth-scripted-car-physics/3915628

### Risks
- IP / TRADEMARK: Do not use 'Grand Theft Auto', 'GTA', Rockstar or Take-Two names, logos, fonts or recognizable map or character likeness, or real car or gun brand names and logos, in the title, thumbnails or assets. That risks DMCA takedown and moderation. Use an original name (e.g. a fictional city) and fictional vehicle makes.
- UNCERTAIN – client-to-server replication of VehicleSeat.ThrottleFloat/SteerFloat: the API dump shows these float properties as replicated (int Throttle/Steer are NotReplicated), but I did not confirm that writes made by the occupant's client reach the server. Design the controller to run on the owning client and read input locally. If the server needs driver input, send it via UnreliableRemoteEvent or InputActions, and test in a live server.
- UNCERTAIN – inside RunService:BindToSimulation, only 'Simulation Access' properties may be written. I did not confirm that VectorForce.Force and BasePart.AssemblyAngularVelocity/AssemblyLinearVelocity carry that capability. If they error, fall back to PreSimulation, or store results in attributes and apply them in PostSimulation, as the docs suggest.
- UNCERTAIN – StreamingIntegrityMode=PauseOutsideLoadedArea (the docs' recommendation) may visibly pause fast cars at 130–170 studs/s on slow connections. Test it against MinimumRadiusPause, and raise StreamingMinRadius (128–256) for driving.
- UNCERTAIN – the tuning numbers (k≈68, c≈4, 45 studs/s² accel, Vmax 130, gripRate 10, yaw 2.4 rad/s, AlignOrientation MaxTorque≈M*400) come from analytic derivation and arcade conventions, not a tested template. Expect a playtest tuning pass. Read AssemblyMass at runtime because welded Massless parts change it.
- Server Authority went to full release in July 2026 but has open issues: BodyMover/constraint drift on vehicles, about 6.25x resimulation cost, remotes off the shared timeline, mobile and console client lag after updates, and a 64-attribute cap. Enabling it later would require reworking the car and input code. Keep that code as pure step functions to make migration possible.
- Rojo version risk: the rbx-dom master reflection DB (v0.741) can serialize AuthorityMode, UseFixedSimulation, PlayerScriptsUseInputActionSystem, SignalBehavior and other streaming properties. An older released Rojo binary may bundle an older DB lacking some of them. Pin a recent Rojo and verify the built .rbxl, for example with a Luau Execution smoke test that prints workspace.StreamingEnabled.
- Highlight cap: the official doc page now says 255 and a Roblox Rendering post confirms the raise, but older cached docs and forum posts say 31. Each Highlight still costs a draw call plus post-processing, which matters on mobile.
- Built-in rbxasset:// list comes from the Studio build manifest (0.744). Mobile and console clients ship the same content folder but this was not verified per platform. Whether ParticleEmitter.Texture accepts .dds rbxasset paths was not verified; prefer .png built-ins or uploaded image IDs for particles.
- Sound IDs are verified public today, but Roblox has previously re-homed and privatized audio (2022 audio privacy update; partner accounts created Oct 2024). Centralize all IDs in one Sounds config module so swapping is trivial, and preload with ContentProvider:PreloadAsync.
- ProfileStore license was not confirmed from the source fetched; check the repo LICENSE before vendoring. The Wally package '2jammers/profilestore' is community-published, not by loleris. Prefer vendoring the official GitHub source.
- BindReceiptHandler / Enum.ReceiptDecision appear in the current MarketplaceService reference and API dump. However, the developer-products guide still shows only ProcessReceipt with Enum.ProductPurchaseDecision. Use ProcessReceipt (well-trodden) unless BindReceiptHandler is verified in a live test.
- Content maturity: the questionnaire does not specifically address crime themes (car theft, robbery, police chases) or in-game guns. Violence 'realism' is judged by reviewers, so humanoid shooting with ragdolls could be rated Moderate even without blood, which shrinks the reachable audience. Keep weapons stylized and outcomes non-lethal (KO/Busted) to stay Mild.
- Paid random items: free-currency crates are a gray area in DevForum discussion. If a currency can be bought with Robux anywhere in the game, treat every crate bought with it as a paid random item that needs odds disclosure and PolicyService gating.
- Client-local traffic proxies make cross-player collisions inconsistent: player A's crash into a traffic car is resolved locally, and other players only see the server-broadcast result about 200 ms later. That is acceptable for ambient traffic but not for mission-critical vehicles, which should be real server-spawned cars.
